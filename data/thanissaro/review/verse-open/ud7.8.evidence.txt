# ud7.8
Source: data/upstream/thanissaro/sutta/kn/ud/vagga7/ud7.8.html

SOURCE 001 prose: I have heard that on one occasion the Blessed One was staying near Sāvatthī at Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. Now at that time Ven. Mahā Kaccāna was sitting not far from the Blessed One, his legs crossed, his body held erect, having mindfulness immersed in the body well-established to the fore within. The Blessed One saw Ven. Mahā Kaccāna sitting not far away, his legs crossed, his body held erect, having mindfulness immersed in the body well-established to the fore within.
SOURCE 002 prose: Then, on realizing the significance of that, the Blessed One on that occasion exclaimed:
SOURCE 003 verse: If one were to have
SOURCE 004 verse: mindfulness always
SOURCE 005 verse: established, continually
SOURCE 006 verse: immersed in the body,
SOURCE 007 verse: (thinking,)
SOURCE 008 verse: “It should not be,
SOURCE 009 verse: it should not be mine;
SOURCE 010 verse: it will not be,
SOURCE 011 verse: it will not be mine”–
SOURCE 012 verse: there,
SOURCE 013 verse: in that step-by-step dwelling,
SOURCE 014 verse: one in no long time
SOURCE 015 verse: would cross over
SOURCE 016 verse: attachment.
SOURCE 017 prose: In AN 10:29, the Buddha recommends this view as conducive to developing dispassion for becoming. However, in MN 106 he warns that it can lead to the refined equanimity of the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception, which can become an object of clinging. Only if that subtle clinging is detected can all clinging be abandoned.
SOURCE 018 prose: The Canon’s most extended discussion of this theme of meditation is in SN 22:55. See Appendix Two.
SOURCE 019 prose: For more on this topic, see The Paradox of Becoming, chapter 5.
