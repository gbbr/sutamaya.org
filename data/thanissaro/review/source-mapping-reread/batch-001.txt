an5.254:0.3 | P: Pañcamacchariyasutta | S: Five Kinds of Stinginess | T: Stinginess
an5.254:1.1 | P: “Pañcimāni, bhikkhave, macchariyāni. | S: “Mendicants, there are these five kinds of stinginess. | T: “Monks, there are these five forms of stinginess.
an5.254:1.2 | P: Katamāni pañca? | S: What five? | T: Which five?
an5.254:1.3 | P: Āvāsamacchariyaṁ, kulamacchariyaṁ, lābhamacchariyaṁ, vaṇṇamacchariyaṁ, dhammamacchariyaṁ— | S: Stinginess with dwellings, families, material things, praise, and the teachings. | T: Stinginess as to one’s monastery (lodgings), stinginess as to one’s family (of supporters), stinginess as to one’s gains, stinginess as to one’s status, and stinginess as to the Dhamma.
an5.254:1.4 | P: imāni kho, bhikkhave, pañca macchariyāni. | S: These are the five kinds of stinginess. | T: These are the five forms of stinginess.
an5.254:1.5 | P: Imesaṁ kho, bhikkhave, pañcannaṁ macchariyānaṁ etaṁ paṭikuṭṭhaṁ, yadidaṁ dhammamacchariyan”ti. | S: The most contemptible of these five kinds of stinginess is stinginess with the teaching.” | T: And the meanest of these five is this: stinginess as to the Dhamma.”
