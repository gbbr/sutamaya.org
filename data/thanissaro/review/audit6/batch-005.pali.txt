sn17.5:1.1 | P: Sāvatthiyaṁ viharati. | S: At Sāvatthī. | T: Staying near Sāvatthī.
sn17.5:1.2 | P: “Dāruṇo, bhikkhave, lābhasakkārasiloko …pe… adhigamāya. | S: “Possessions, honor, and popularity are grim … | T: “Monks, gains, offerings, & fame are a cruel thing, a harsh, bitter obstacle to the attainment of the unexcelled rest from bondage.
sn17.5:1.3 | P: Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, mīḷhakā gūthādī gūthapūrā puṇṇā gūthassa. Purato cassa mahāgūthapuñjo. | S: Suppose there was a dung-eating beetle full of dung, stuffed with dung, and before her was a big pile of dung. | T: Suppose there were a beetle, a dung-eater, full of dung, gorged with dung, with a huge pile of dung in front of him.
sn17.5:1.4 | P: Sā tena aññā mīḷhakā atimaññeyya: | S: She’d look down on other beetles, thinking: | T: He, because of that, would look down on other beetles:
sn17.5:1.5 | P: ‘ahamhi gūthādī gūthapūrā puṇṇā gūthassa, purato ca myāyaṁ mahāgūthapuñjo’ti. | S: ‘For I am a dung-eating beetle full of dung, stuffed with dung, and before me is a big pile of dung.’ | T: ‘Yes, sirree! I am a dung-eater, full of dung, gorged with dung, with a huge pile of dung in front of me!’
sn17.5:1.6 | P: Evameva kho, bhikkhave, idhekacco bhikkhu lābhasakkārasilokena abhibhūto pariyādiṇṇacitto pubbaṇhasamayaṁ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya gāmaṁ vā nigamaṁ vā piṇḍāya pavisati. | S: In the same way, take a certain mendicant whose mind is overcome and overwhelmed by possessions, honor, and popularity. They robe up in the morning and, taking their bowl and robe, enter the village or town for alms. | T: In the same way, there is the case where a certain monk—conquered by gains, offerings, & fame, his mind consumed—adjusts his lower robe and, taking his bowl & outer robe, goes into a village or town for alms.
sn17.5:1.7 | P: So tattha bhuttāvī ca hoti yāvadattho, nimantito ca svātanāya, piṇḍapāto cassa pūro. | S: There they eat as much as they like, get invited back tomorrow, and have plenty of almsfood. | T: Having eaten there as much as he likes—full of almsfood & invited again for the next day—
sn17.5:1.8 | P: So ārāmaṁ gantvā bhikkhugaṇassa majjhe vikatthati: | S: When they get back to the monastery, they boast in the middle of a group of mendicants: | T: he goes to the monastery and, in the midst of a group of monks, boasts,
sn17.5:1.9 | P: ‘bhuttāvī camhi yāvadattho, nimantito camhi svātanāya, piṇḍapāto ca myāyaṁ pūro, lābhī camhi cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārānaṁ, ime panaññe bhikkhū appapuññā appesakkhā na lābhino cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārānan’ti. | S: ‘I ate as much as I liked, got invited back tomorrow, and had plenty of almsfood. I get robes, almsfood, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick. But these other mendicants have little merit or significance, so they don’t get these things.’ | T: ‘I have eaten as much as I like, I am full of almsfood & have been invited again for tomorrow. I am a recipient of robes, almsfood, lodgings, & medicinal requisites for curing illness. These other monks, though, have next to no merit, next to no influence. They aren’t recipients of robes, almsfood, lodgings, & medicinal requisites for curing illness.’
sn17.5:1.10 | P: So tena lābhasakkārasilokena abhibhūto pariyādiṇṇacitto aññe pesale bhikkhū atimaññati. | S: With a mind overcome and overwhelmed by possessions, honor, and popularity, they look down on other good-hearted mendicants. | T: Conquered by gains, offerings, & fame, his mind consumed, he looks down on other well-behaved monks.
sn17.5:1.11 | P: Tañhi tassa, bhikkhave, moghapurisassa hoti dīgharattaṁ ahitāya dukkhāya. | S: This will be for their lasting harm and suffering. | T: That will be for this worthless man’s long-term suffering & harm.
sn17.5:1.12 | P: Evaṁ dāruṇo kho, bhikkhave, lābhasakkārasiloko …pe… | S: So grim are possessions, honor, and popularity. …” | T: That’s how cruel gains, offerings, & fame are: a harsh, bitter obstacle to the attainment of the unexcelled rest from bondage. “So you should train yourselves: ‘We will put aside any gains, offerings, & fame that have arisen; and we will not let any gains, offerings, & fame that have arisen keep our minds consumed.’
