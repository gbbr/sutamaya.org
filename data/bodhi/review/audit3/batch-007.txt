an10.11:2.2 | S: It’s when a noble disciple has faith in the Realized One’s awakening: | T: (1) “Here, a bhikkhu is endowed with faith. He has faith in the enlightenment of the Tathāgata thus:

an10.11:2.4 | S: They are rarely ill or unwell. Their stomach digests well, being neither too hot nor too cold, but just right, and fit for meditation. | T: (2) “He is seldom ill or afflicted, possessing an even digestion that is neither too cool nor too hot but moderate and suitable for striving.
an10.11:2.5 | S: They’re not devious or deceitful. They reveal themselves honestly to the Teacher or sensible spiritual companions. | T: (3) “He is honest and open, one who reveals himself as he really is to the Teacher and his wise fellow monks.
an10.11:2.6 | S: They live with energy roused up for giving up unskillful qualities and embracing skillful qualities. They’re strong, staunchly vigorous, not slacking off when it comes to developing skillful qualities. | T: (4) “He has aroused energy for abandoning unwholesome qualities and acquiring wholesome qualities; he is strong, firm in exertion, not casting off the duty of cultivating wholesome qualities.
an10.11:2.7 | S: They’re wise. They have the wisdom of arising and passing away which is noble, penetrative, and leads to the complete ending of suffering. | T: (5) “He is wise; he possesses the wisdom that discerns arising and passing away, which is noble and penetrative and leads to the complete destruction of suffering.
an10.11:2.8 | S: That’s how a mendicant has five factors. | T: “It is in this way that a bhikkhu possesses five factors.
an10.11:3.1 | S: And how does a lodging have five factors? | T: “And how does a lodging possess five factors?
an10.11:3.2 | S: It’s when a lodging is neither too far nor too near, but convenient for coming and going. | T: (6) “Here, the lodging is neither too far from a place for alms nor too close, and it possesses a means for going and returning.
an10.11:3.3 | S: It’s not bothered by people by day, and at night it’s quiet and still. | T: (7) “During the day it is not disturbed by people and at night it is quiet and still.
an10.11:3.4 | S: There’s little disturbance from flies, mosquitoes, wind, sun, and reptiles. | T: (8) “There is little contact with flies, mosquitoes, wind, the burning sun, and serpents.
an10.11:3.5 | S: While staying in that lodging the necessities of life—robes, almsfood, lodgings, and medicines and supplies for the sick—are easy to come by. | T: (9) “One dwelling in that lodging can easily obtain robes, almsfood, lodging, and medicines and provisions for the sick.
an10.11:3.6 | S: And in that lodging there are several senior mendicants who are very learned, inheritors of the heritage, who have memorized the teachings, the monastic law, and the outlines. | T: (10) “In that lodging elder bhikkhus are dwelling who are learned, heirs to the heritage, experts on the Dhamma, experts on the discipline, experts on the outlines.

an10.11:3.9 | S: Those venerables reveal what is hidden, clarify what is unclear, and dispel doubt regarding the many doubtful matters. | T: Those venerable ones then disclose to him what has not been disclosed, clear up what is obscure, and dispel his perplexity about numerous perplexing points.
an10.11:3.10 | S: That’s how a lodging has five factors. | T: “It is in this way that a lodging possesses five factors.
