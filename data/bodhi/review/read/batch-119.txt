## sn56.49
sn56.49:1.1 | S: “Mendicants, suppose a person was to place on Sineru, the king of mountains, seven pebbles the size of mung beans. | T: “Bhikkhus, suppose that a man would place on Sineru, the king of mountains, seven grains of gravel the size of mung beans.
sn56.49:1.2 | S: What do you think, mendicants? | T: What do you think, bhikkhus,
sn56.49:1.3 | S: Which is more: the seven pebbles the size of mung beans? Or Sineru, the king of mountains?” | T: which is more: the seven grains of gravel the size of mung beans that have been placed there or Sineru, the king of mountains?”
sn56.49:1.4 | S: “Sir, Sineru, the king of mountains, is certainly more. | T: “Venerable sir, Sineru, the king of mountains, is more.
sn56.49:1.5 | S: The seven pebbles the size of mung beans are tiny. | T: The seven grains of gravel the size of mung beans are trifling.
sn56.49:1.6 | S: Compared to Sineru, they don’t count, there’s no comparison, they’re not worth a fraction.” | T: Compared to Sineru, the king of mountains, the seven grains of gravel the size of mung beans are not calculable, do not bear comparison, do not amount even to a fraction.”
sn56.49:1.7 | S: “In the same way, for an individual with comprehension, a noble disciple accomplished in view, the suffering that’s over and done with is more, what’s left is tiny. | T: “So too, bhikkhus, for a noble disciple, a person accomplished in view who has made the breakthrough, the suffering that has been utterly destroyed and eliminated is more, while that which remains is trifling.

sn56.49:2.1 | S: That’s why you should practice meditation …” | T: “Therefore, bhikkhus, an exertion should be made to understand: ‘This is suffering.’… An exertion should be made to understand: ‘This is the way leading to the cessation of suffering.’”
