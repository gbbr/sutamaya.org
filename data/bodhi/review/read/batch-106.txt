sn22.7:9.4 | S: Anxieties—born of latching on to the perishing of consciousness and originating in accordance with natural principles—don’t occupy their mind. | T: No agitation and constellation of mental states born of preoccupation with the change of consciousness remain obsessing his mind.
sn22.7:9.5 | S: So they don’t become frightened, worried, concerned, or anxious because of grasping. | T: Because his mind is not obsessed, he is not frightened, distressed, or anxious, and through nonclinging he does not become agitated.
sn22.7:9.6 | S: That’s how not grasping leads to freedom from anxiety.” | T: “It is in such a way, bhikkhus, that there is nonagitation through nonclinging.”
